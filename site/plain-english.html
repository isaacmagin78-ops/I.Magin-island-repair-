Plain English

Every word that gets used at you, in one sentence each — and what yours actually is. You learned this from videos once and stopped watching for two months. This is the same list, written down, so you never have to re-learn it from a video again.

01Where your work lives

repo

A folder of your work that remembers every version of every file, forever. Nothing in it is ever really deleted.

Yours is I.Magin-island-repair-. The name is left over from an app that was never built. Ignore the name.

git

The machine that does the remembering. When people say "it's in git," they mean it cannot be lost.

This is why nothing you built is actually gone, even on the days it felt gone.

GitHub

The website where a copy of your repo lives, so it isn't only on one computer.

Your account is isaacmagin78-ops.

main

The one official copy. The real version.

If something isn't on main, no future session will ever see it. This one sentence explains most of the last two months.

branch

A side copy for work in progress, so unfinished work doesn't disturb the real version.

Work left on a branch and never merged is invisible. That is exactly how a month "disappeared" — it was sitting on a branch nobody looked at.

commit

Saving a snapshot, with a note saying what changed and why.

Every note I write is aimed at whoever reads it in six months — often you.

push

Sending your saved snapshots up to GitHub. Until you push, they only exist locally.

When I say "pushed," it means it's safe and everyone can see it.

02What I am, and why we keep contradicting each other

session

One conversation with Claude Code. It starts knowing nothing about any other conversation.

Sessions cannot see each other. Ever. That is the shape of the tool, not a bug and not a memory problem.

the files are the memory

Not a technical term — the workaround. Because sessions forget, everything important gets written into the repo instead of said in chat.

This is why I keep saying "written down and pushed." That's me handing the next session what I know.

connector / MCP

A wire from Claude to another company's service. With the wire, I can see your real account. Without it, I'm guessing.

This is why one Claude can do something another Claude can't. Not one lying — different wires plugged in.

cloud session vs. device

A cloud session runs on Anthropic's computers with a copy of your repo. A device is your actual MacBook.

Everything you've talked to has been the cloud kind. That's why nobody could touch your Mac.

03Getting something in front of a person

artifact

A web page Claude publishes for you at a claude.ai link. Fast, free, and private until you share it from the page's own share menu.

The restaurant sample page and the Croatia route page are both artifacts.

deploy

Putting a page on the open internet at an address you own, that works whether or not anyone is signed into anything.

This is the part that's been stuck. Not because it's hard — because of one wrong login.

Vercel

A company that hosts websites. You point it at your repo and it puts the pages online.

Your real account is under isaacmagin78@gmail.com. The connector was pointed at a different, empty one for four weeks.

QR code

A picture of a link. Nothing more. A phone camera reads the picture and opens the link.

A QR pointing at a private page shows a stranger a login wall — which is why every one of yours gets scanned on a second phone before it's printed.

04Things that run without you

the watcher

A small program that runs on a timer on GitHub's computers, with nobody logged in and nothing open.

Yours checks your creators and Hacker News twice a day and writes what's new into WATCH-FEED.md. It exists because Buzz reached you through a friend three weeks late.

agent

Any program that does several steps on its own instead of waiting for you at each one.

The watcher is one. The two ElevenLabs voices you set up in April are two more — they can talk, but no phone number reaches them.

None of this is intelligence. It's vocabulary.

You built everything here for six or seven months on a phone, with none of these words. The words came later, from other people, faster than anyone could absorb them — and then everyone kept using them as if you'd agreed to.

You don't have to remember this page. It stays in your repo. Ask for it by name and any session will bring it back.
