## Why cameras change the economics

Pet content can't be directed. With one camera you either catch the moment or
you don't, and most days you don't. That's why the Tyson's Time queue keeps
running dry — supply depends on somebody happening to be filming.

Always-on multi-camera inverts it. The moment is always captured, from at least
one usable angle, and having a second angle is what makes a 15-second clip feel
*cut* rather than *found*. Two angles of the same three seconds is an edit. One
angle is a home video.

**This is the supply fix for the posting queue, not just a new show.**

## Buying guide

Priority order — these matter more than brand.

1. **4K, not 1080p.** Reels are vertical; these cameras shoot horizontal.
   Cropping 1080p to vertical yields ~600px wide and looks bad on a phone.
   Cropping 4K to vertical still yields a full 1080p vertical frame.
2. **Continuous recording to local storage (SD card), not motion-triggered
   clips.** Motion detection fires *after* movement starts, so it clips the
   first second — which is the hook. Continuous recording lets you scrub back
   and take the whole moment. It also avoids per-camera cloud fees.
3. **60fps if available.** Slow motion is disproportionately effective on animal
   content and you can't add it later.
4. **Outdoor/weather rated.** It's a balcony in South Florida.
5. Match the existing brand if convenient — one app beats four apps — but don't
   trade down to 1080p to stay in an ecosystem.

Audio is not worth optimizing for. Balcony wind ruins it; plan on music and VO.

## Camera placement, in priority order

Place in this order regardless of how many end up bought.

| # | Position | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Along the railing**, shooting down the rail line so the cat walks toward or past the lens | The money shot. This is the brand. |
| 2 | **Wide, including the drop** | Makes "eight stories up" a visual instead of a claim. Without this the hook doesn't land. |
| 3 | **Doorway, shooting from inside out** | Catches entrances — the "Enter Tyson" beat — and gives reaction coverage. |
| 4 | **Floor level, low angle** | Makes a small cat look monumental; puts Tyson at eye level. |

Watch sun position. A balcony camera aimed at open sky will silhouette both
animals for part of the day. Check the frame at the hours they're actually out
there before committing to a mount.

## Formats a fixed rig produces on its own

These only work with locked-off cameras, which is the point of mounting rather
than handholding.

- **The Morning Patrol** — same time, same angle, daily. The repetition *is* the
  format; viewers come back for the constant.
- **Same frame, different day** — montage of one fixed shot over weeks. Only
  possible with a locked camera. Highly shareable.
- **The two-shot** — cat and dog in one frame. It's called *Buddies*; current
  assets treat them mostly separately. Multi-cam finally covers the interaction.

## Distribution

Don't launch a channel cold. Tyson's Time already has real distribution —
Instagram is the 10x channel at ~2,200 views/reel. Run crossover clips there
first and let them seed a dedicated Balcony Buddies channel, rather than
starting a new audience from zero.

## Two flags

- **Peril reads as peril.** A cat on a railing eight stories up will draw a
  comment section arguing about safety, and platforms sometimes limit reach on
  content that reads as an animal in danger. Not a reason to drop it — it's part
  of why the footage is compelling — but expect it, and decide in advance
  whether to address it in captions or ignore it.
- **203 Calico is Emilio's cat.** The archived plan includes merch lines and a
  children's book. Whatever the arrangement with Emilio is, settle it before
  there's revenue attached. Cheap to sort now, expensive later.
