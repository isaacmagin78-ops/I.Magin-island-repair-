Part 1

The Story

Tyson is a real rescue pit bull. He was returned by his first family and spent two years in a kennel before coming home. A few months ago a tiny rescue kitten named Miss moved in — and everyone said that was a mistake. Instead, the 70-pound dog everyone feared became the kitten's gentlest friend.

That's not a marketing angle. It's documented daily on video, and it's the engine of everything in this packet.

Why this works commercially

  • Pet content is the strongest category on social media — and "unlikely animal friendships" (a feared dog + a fearless kitten) is its most shareable sub-genre.
  • The pit bull redemption story is a cultural movement — audiences actively seek content that flips the breed stereotype.
  • The character already exists. Most children's brands have to invent their hero. Tyson is real, filmed daily, and already has a story arc people cry over.

The one-sentence pitch: Bluey started as a cartoon and became a billion-dollar brand. Tyson starts as a real dog — the book, the videos, and the products all grow from an animal people can actually meet, follow, and love.

The 6-Month Plan

Months 1–2 — Grow

  • Daily story-first shorts (already automated); kit sold via bio and pinned comments
  • Cross the 500-subscriber YouTube threshold → native ad revenue switches on
  • Lock character art designs for the book (Tyson, Miss, Walker)

Months 2–4 — Illustrate & Launch the Book

  • Commission the book's illustrations (the single biggest use of funds)
  • Publish on Amazon KDP: print-on-demand, zero inventory, ~$5–8 profit per copy
  • Launch to the audience as "the story you've been watching, now a real book"

Months 4–6 — Multiply

  • Animated shorts made from book pages (AI pipeline already selected)
  • Read-along videos for YouTube; second product ladder begins (coloring book, stickers)
