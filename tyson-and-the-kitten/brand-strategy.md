## Character Hierarchy

### Tier 1: Tyson (The Heart)
- Real rescue pit bull. Authentic. The reason people will care.
- Visual identity: warm, soulful eyes. Big head. Gentle giant energy.
- The "real Tyson vs. storybook Tyson" content format is the secret weapon.

### Tier 2: The Kitten (The Breakout Star)
- Tiny, fearless, bossy. Rides Tyson. Sleeps on Tyson. Orders Tyson around.
- Kids LOVE tiny fearless characters (Puss in Boots, Stitch, Grogu, Dug).
- The kitten is the merchandise engine. Plush kitten. Kitten stickers. Kitten T-shirts.

### Tier 3: Walker (The Guide)
- Wise, but reduced screen time. Adults love philosophy; kids love funny.
- Walker appears at exactly the moment Tyson truly needs guidance.
- Think Rafiki from The Lion King: brief, impactful, memorable.

### Tier 4: The Grumblers (Comic Relief)
- Not villains. Ridiculous. Children remember funny far more than mean.
- So absurd that kids laugh at them, not fear them.
- Visual gag: small, grumpy creatures who are secretly soft.

## Visual Production Standard: "Every Page Is a Scene"

### The Core Principle

Each spread must be designed as if it were a **5–10 second animated scene**, with a character entering or reacting, a clear emotional beat, a camera angle that serves the story, lighting that supports the mood, and a moment that could be silent and still convey everything.

### Style Direction
- **Primary style**: Pixar-meets-watercolor — 3D character rendering with painterly backgrounds.
- **Color palette**: Warm earth tones for Tyson (amber, rust, cream). Cool bright tones for the kitten (white, light blue, soft gray). Muted greens/browns for Walker's world. Clashing, muddy colors for the Grumblers.
- **Lighting**: Golden hour warmth for emotional scenes. Soft diffused light for tender moments. Funny-dramatic shadows for the Grumblers, never scary-dramatic.

### Frame Language (Cinematic)

| Scene Type | Framing | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Tyson alone | Wide shot, small in frame | Isolation, misunderstood |
| Tyson + kitten meeting | Extreme close-up, eye level | Intimacy, the moment everything changes |
| Kitten being bossy | Low angle, looking up at kitten | Kitten feels bigger than Tyson (comedic) |
| Grumblers | Dutch angle, slightly distorted | Absurdity, comedy |
| Walker | Medium shot, warm light | Wisdom, calm, safety |
| Tyson running/playing | Tracking shot, motion blur | Freedom, joy, release |

### Wordless Pages
- At least **4–6 spreads with no text** — just Tyson, just the kitten, just expressions.
- Wordless pages become the most shareable video content.

### Character Consistency Rules
- Lock Tyson's character design before generating any images.
- Character reference sheet for every AI generation. Same for the kitten, Walker, each Grumbler.

## Visual Bible: Non-Negotiables

### The Silent-Film Test
Every page must communicate its full emotional beat with **zero text**.

### The Thumbnail Test
Every scene must read clearly at **phone-screen size**. Big shapes. Clear silhouettes. Readable expressions.

### The Three-Frame Animatic Rule
Each spread must have a **beginning, middle, and end frame** — three distinct visual moments animatable as a 6–12 second sequence: Setup → Action → Resolution.

### Character Bible Requirements

| Element | Tyson | Kitten | Walker | Grumblers |

### Video Export Specification

| Spec | Requirement |
|------|-----------|
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical (1080×1920) |
| Duration | 6–12 seconds per scene |
| Hook | First 1–2 seconds must grab attention |

## Launch Sequence

### Phase 0: Foundation (Weeks 1–4)
Character lock; reference sheets; social accounts; film 20–30 real-Tyson clips; first 5 "real → illustrated" posts; "Join Tyson's Pack" email capture.

### Phase 1: Social Media Presence (Weeks 4–12)
Daily posting. Real Tyson focus. **No selling.** Build to 1,000 engaged followers before mentioning any product. Mix: 70% real / 20% illustrated / 10% process. Email list via free coloring pages.

### Success Metrics

| Metric | What to Watch | When to Act |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Top hooks | Which first 1–2s get views | Double down; retire weak after 3 tries |
| Shares & saves | Saves = bookmark-worthy; shares = resonance | Saves > 5% of views = keeper |
| Email signups | Social → owned audience | Target 5% of engaged followers |
| Follower growth | Weekly net new | Sustained 5%+ weekly = ready to sell |
| Comment themes | Repeated characters/moments | Become priority pages/plush/merch |
| Watch time | Full 6–12s completion? | Drop-off before 3s = hook failure |

### Phase 2: Animated Shorts (Weeks 8–16)
10–20s AI-animated scenes, standalone, no CTA. Best-performing scenes = the book's best pages.

### Phase 3: Book Launch (Weeks 16–20)
"The story everyone's been watching come to life." Pre-sell limited first edition; sticker sheet with first 500; email list first access.

### Phase 4: Merchandise Ladder (Weeks 20+)
1. Plush Tyson ($30) → 2. Plush kitten ($22) → 3. Coloring book ($10) → 4. Activity book ($12) → 5. T-shirts ($28) → 6. Sticker packs ($8) → 7. Dog toys ($20) → 8. Teacher guide (free) → 9. School visits.

### Phase 5: Licensing & Partnerships (Months 6–12)
Amazon storefront; rescue-org partnerships; co-branded products; licensing conversations; bilingual edition (EN/ES).
