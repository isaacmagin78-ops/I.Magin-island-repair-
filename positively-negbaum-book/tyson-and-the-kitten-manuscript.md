# Positively Tyson
## Tyson and the Kitten
### Master Manuscript + Illustration Guide — adapted from *Positively Negbaum* ("Good, Better, Best" master draft)

**Canon spine (carried over from Book #1):**
Tyson is judged by his name and his breed before anyone asks who he is. The Grumble Pack tries to drag him into "Who's Got It Worse?" The meanest voice ends up inside his own head. Miss the Kitten helps him get quiet enough to hear Walker. Walker teaches him that choices shape fate, not a name. Tyson flips the game to "Good, Better, Best" and reclaims his name. Miss's fearlessness is seeded as both strength and limitation (her Happy-the-Clam role), saved for Book #2.

## Back Cover Copy

Tyson is the most misunderstood dog on the whole wide block.

The neighborhood hears his name and decides he must be trouble. Then the Grumble Pack joins the fray — spreading rumors, stirring the street, and trying to pull Tyson into their favorite game: **Who's Got It Worse?**

But with help from Miss the Kitten and old Walker the Wise, Tyson learns that names do not steer dogs. **Choices do.**

Soon Tyson changes the game from blame to gratitude with **Good, Better, Best** — proving that the same name they flinched at before can become the name the whole block comes looking for.

Because your choices shape your fate, not your name.
And a good dog walks right through the blame.

## Core Moral

**Child-facing:**
You can't choose every thought that flies by,
but you can choose which thoughts you feed.

**Parent-facing:**
Children may inherit labels, reputations, fears, and language from family, breed, or block — but they are not trapped by them. With quiet, truth, gratitude, wise guidance, and one fearless friend, they can notice negative self-talk and practice a better way.

## Parent / Teacher Discussion Questions

1. Why did the block judge Tyson before knowing him?
2. What did the Grumble Pack want Tyson to believe?
3. Why did Tyson start to believe them? Where was the meanest voice really coming from?
4. How did Miss help Tyson? Why is quiet sometimes useful?
5. What did Walker mean when he said, "The obstacle is the way"?
6. How did Tyson change the game? What is one thing *you* are grateful for — can you make it Good, Better, Best?
7. What does it mean to own your name?
8. Miss is brave — when is brave a gift, and when does brave need a friend's advice?
9. Who helps you stay on the good path?

## Series Notes

**Locked by this draft:**

- Miss's quiet place is the purr; Tyson can't copy it and learns his own version (breathe, let thought-birds pass).
- Tyson flips Who's Got It Worse into Good, Better, Best.
- Tyson reclaims his name: "I'm positively Tyson."
- Miss's fearlessness seeded as both strength and limitation.
