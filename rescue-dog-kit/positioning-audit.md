## The headline finding

**Positioning is not why this isn't selling. Distribution is.**

The kit has **25 checkout sessions all-time** and **zero external customers**. The one
charge on record is Isaac's own self-test (2026-07-24). Nothing since 07-31.

You cannot diagnose a conversion problem at that volume — there is no traffic to
convert. The page is fine. Almost nobody has ever seen it.

### Where the funnel is actually broken

| Leak | Detail |
|---|---|
| **33 YouTube videos, 11,929 views, no links in descriptions** | The single largest one. An audience already exists and is handed no way to buy. |
| **Posting queue empty** | 0 scheduled. All channels dark since Aug 10. |
| **Instagram dark** | Your best channel by ~10x (~2,200 views / 1,500 reach) is publishing nothing. |
| **Only one affiliate link ever created** | ~25 posts carry the Amazon disclosure with no link in the funnel. |
| **Two posts published broken Aug 8** | Literal caption `Post Text`, no Kit link, no disclosure. |

The kit's infrastructure works end to end — page, Stripe checkout, PDF, automated
delivery. It has been verified. It is simply not connected to the audience that exists.

## What the page already gets right

Reading the live copy, the page independently does most of what a competitive
repositioning would have recommended. This is a genuinely well-positioned product:

- **Owns rescue-specific language.** "Decompression & Adjustment Schedule" is right
  there in the contents. No puppy-language leakage.
- **Leads with the emotional truth, not features.** *"The bowl goes untouched… you're
  googling at 2am wondering if you're doing this wrong. You're not doing it wrong.
  You're doing it without a plan."* That is the actual buying emotion in the category.
- **Has an unfakeable origin story.** *"Tyson is a real rescue, and this kit is his real
  first month. No stock dogs, no invented experts."* Competitors cannot copy this.
- **Names the gift-giver as a second buyer** — the purchase occasion the course
  competitors structurally cannot serve.
- **Scopes honestly.** The "who it's not for" block and the trainer/vet referral build
  trust rather than overpromising.

**Corrections to the blind draft:** H2 (puppy-language leakage) was wrong — the page
uses rescue language correctly throughout. H5 (collision with free 3-3-3 PDFs) is
weaker than stated — the kit sells *sequencing and trackers*, not the information
itself, which is the right answer to free alternatives.

## Real positioning findings, in priority order

### 1. "All sales are final" is friction at the decision moment — and it is beatable

The page says: *"Because it's an instantly delivered digital file, all sales are final."*

SpiritDog — a direct competitor explicitly targeting "dogs with a tough past" — wins on
**lifetime access plus a 30-day money-back guarantee.** Risk reversal is close to
mandatory for information products, and it costs almost nothing on a $19 digital file:
refund requests at that price point are rare, and the guarantee's job is to remove
hesitation before the click, not to be exercised.

**24 of 25 checkout sessions expired unpaid.** The sample is far too small to be
conclusive, but 100% abandonment on a $19 impulse purchase is at least consistent with
last-second hesitation. A 30-day guarantee is the cheapest available test.

### 2. The price signals "leaflet," not "program"

$19 sits far below Camp B (courses routinely $100+) and just below Chewy's $49
free-shipping line, which is the anchor for anything that looks like dog gear. The
content — roadmap, schedules, trackers, logs, question sheets — is program-shaped, but
$19 invites the buyer to read it as a PDF leaflet.

This is *not* the reason for zero sales and should not be touched until traffic exists.
Flagging it as a later test, not a fix.

### 3. "30 Days" is the category's most crowded number

At least four competitors sell a 30-day container (SpiritDog, Udemy's 30 Day Challenge,
30 Things to Teach Your Dog in 30 Days, Rescued by Training). Meanwhile the framework
rescue adopters actually think in is **3-3-3** — 3 days to decompress, 3 weeks to settle,
3 months to feel at home — which every rescue org, shelter, and blog teaches, and which
**no product commercially owns.**

The kit already contains the decompression logic. It just isn't named for the framework
its buyers arrive already believing. Worth testing on the headline once there is enough
traffic to read a result — not before.

## The competitive landscape

The category splits in two, and the gap between them is where this kit sits.

**Camp A — gear bundles** (Chewy, Petco, Ruffwear, PuppySpot, EntirelyPets). Boxes of
objects in puppy language, anchored to Chewy's $49 free-shipping line. Commodity; every
item is cheaper individually elsewhere.

**Camp B — behavioral programs** (SpiritDog, Grisha Stewart, Udemy, Rescued by
Training). Sell a transformation, compete on risk reversal, no physical object.
SpiritDog and Grisha Stewart are already aimed at rescue adopters specifically — they
are the real competitors.

**The gap:** the rescue adopter's problem is neither "I lack a bowl" nor "I want to
study dog training." It is *"this animal has been here four days, it won't eat, and I'm
afraid I made a mistake."* A transition problem with an emotional core and a hard
deadline. **The First 30 Days Kit is already positioned in this gap.** That is the
finding — the strategy is sound and the execution downstream of it is what's missing.

---

## Recommended sequence

Ordered by value, not by effort. The first two are worth more than every positioning
change combined.

1. **Put Kit links in the 33 YouTube descriptions.** 11,929 existing views, currently
   monetized at zero. Use the sales page, not the raw Stripe link. Amazon disclosure
   required wherever an affiliate link appears.
2. **Refill the posting queue and get Instagram live again.** Per `HANDOFF.md`, scripts
   and footage are both already in hand in Google Drive — 10 ready-to-post packages with
   captions already inside Instagram's 5-hashtag limit. This is not blocked on producing
   anything new.
3. **Fix or delete the two broken Aug 8 posts** (caption `Post Text`, no disclosure).
   The missing disclosure is an Associates compliance exposure, not just a typo.
4. **Add a 30-day guarantee** and remove "all sales are final."
5. *Then*, once traffic is real, test the 3-3-3 headline and the price.
