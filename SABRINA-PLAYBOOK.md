## 🚨 URGENT — a live risk, free to check, and it may explain the flat TikTok

**Blotato's own documentation says a brand-new TikTok account needs a four-week
manual warm-up before you connect any third-party posting tool** — log in daily,
scroll, like, comment, save, follow, and post **once a day by hand**, roughly an
hour a day, for four weeks. Skip it and the account reads as a bot.
**After the warm-up: a maximum of 3 posts a day through third-party tools**, and
you keep engaging manually.

**Isaac's account has 302 followers and has been auto-posted through Blotato.**

Look at what his own analytics say: **30 hours of LIVE built the entire
following, then the line went flat and stayed flat** through two months of
automated posting. The assumption has been that automation simply *didn't help.*
**It is at least as likely that it actively suppressed reach.**

**Check this before anything else. It costs nothing and it is the difference
between a channel that is small and a channel that is throttled.**
*Source: help.blotato.com/platforms/tiktok/brand-new-accounts*

## The production standard — this is the part that should sting

> *"You do not need fancy equipment, fancy editing, or fancy looks."*

She films and edits **everything in the TikTok app itself.** Front camera. **No
external mic.** Trim the silences, add a text title, use TikTok's auto-captions.
**Fifteen minutes per video, maximum.** Short-form is raw and unscripted; only
long-form gets an outline and an editor.

**The whole week is one day.** On Saturday she films **20 short-form videos**,
records **1 long-form YouTube tutorial**, writes **1 newsletter**, and posts a
few promos. One "jampacked caffeinated day."

**Isaac has recycled twelve clips over a month. Her floor is twenty in a day, on
a phone.** The constraint was never equipment.

## The hook rule — the single highest-leverage free change

> *"The #1 thing is copying the first 3 seconds of viral videos in your topic.
> Find the winners, copy their openers, add your twist, post consistently."*

- For your **first 17 posts, only remix proven winners.** Fastest way to build
  intuition about what actually holds attention.
- The on-screen title in the TikTok editor should be **word-for-word the opener
  of the video you are remixing.**
- She notes 99% are clickbait hooks — used because they buy you three seconds.

## Cadence, keywords, hashtags
- TikTok is primary. **3–5 posts a day**, and under 50k followers **wait 1–2
  hours between them.** TikTok doesn't punish volume, so you collect iteration
  data faster. She frames the real bar as **5×/day for a year straight.**
- **2–3 target keywords, each 200k+ search volume**, repeated across title,
  description and captions.
- **Hashtags: 3 broad + 2 niche. Five maximum.**

## Repurposing
Make original content on **1–2 platforms**, repurpose everywhere else. Her
Blotato + n8n automation auto-downloads each new TikTok and cross-posts to
Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Pinterest, Facebook, LinkedIn, Twitter, Threads,
Bluesky and Drive, with per-platform captions, and can turn a video into a quote
card or carousel. **Saves 15+ hours a week — and she still reads every post
before it goes out.**

## "21 Ways to Get Your First 5 Customers ($0 Budget)"
*Published 2026-03-05. 15 free, 6 paid. **17 of 21 recovered** — the rest are a
gap, not a guess.*

**Her instruction: pick 3–4, run them 30 days, double down on the winner.**
**Her #1 if starting from zero: short-form video** — "free, compounds, and feeds
everything else on the list."
**Her sequencing rule:** *"Don't dump your budget into cold outreach before
you've validated your value prop with free methods first."*

### The free ones
1. **Short-form video** — the engine that feeds everything else.
2. **Warm network first.** Family, friends, past coworkers, businesses you've
   worked with. *"Even 1 or 2 warm users gives you a testimonial, a case study,
   and the confidence to go bigger."* **Isaac has zero customers and a thirty-year
   network. This is method two.**
3. **Cold DM — but do the work first.** *"Find someone relevant. Do the work
   upfront. Show them a sample BEFORE asking for anything. Make it dumb simple
   for them to say yes."* Her real example: a guy re-edited one of her videos,
   showed her the before/after, then DM'd. She hired him on the spot.
4. **Upwork** — apply to **50+ jobs in ONE niche**, and be among the first to
   apply. Speed is everything.
5. **A free tool as the front door** — something people already search for.
6. **A free custom GPT as a lead magnet.** She scraped 1,000+ viral TikTok hooks
   into one; thousands use it. About an hour to build.
7. **A half-gated curated list** — 500 items free, the next 500 behind an email.
8. **Podcast guesting.** Go to **listennotes.com**, search your niche, sort by
   size, **email the small and mid-size shows** — they need guests constantly.
   *"12 guest spots build 12 permanent pages."*
9. **Host a free webinar for someone else's audience** — you bring expertise,
   they bring the room.
10. **Template libraries and community forums** — she posts to the n8n workflow
    library, every template linking back. Free, targeted, permanent.
11. **SEO on "competitor + alternatives"** — those searchers are ready to switch.
12. **Directory submissions** (she names SubmitJuice) — set and forget.
13. **Product Hunt** — she names it and flags the honest limit.
14. **An email newsletter** — own the audience you're renting on social.
15. **Long-form tutorials with the product woven in** — problem-first, not
    product-first.

### The paid ones *(3 of 6 recovered)*
16. **Paid ads — start at $10–20/day.** Don't scale until ROI is positive.
17. **Affiliates and micro-influencers.** *"Find micro influencers who understand
    your product and audience, not big names who just want the paycheck."* She
    warns that non-technical influencers produce inauthentic content and negative
    ROI.
18. **Cold email at scale** — **Google Business listings are "a goldmine" for
    local businesses.** You write the value prop; AI personalizes it.

## How she started, and what she regrets
She **validated Blotato publicly on TikTok before building it**, asking whether
people wanted the thing — and asked real people about their pain rather than
asking herself whether the idea was good. The product was **the productized
version of a workflow she was already running for herself.**

Her own sequence: *a 30-second blurry shaky TikTok created demand → an ugly MVP
created proof → early adopters created confusion → launch mistakes created
urgency → fast revenue created conviction to rebuild.*

**Her stated regret: she built the product too soon**, and should have spent
another **6–12 months on content first.** *"Having a personal brand lets you
bootstrap the first $0 to $10k MRR fast."*

**Isaac has a working checkout, a real brand, and 302 followers. Her read of her
own history says the content comes first and the customers follow it — not the
reverse.**
