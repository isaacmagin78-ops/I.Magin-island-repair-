# Getting the company set up

**Written 2026-08-18, when Isaac said: *"if we're actually gonna start
proceeding and start having an income I have to have everything set up in
place."*** He is right, and the timing is right — not because of the $19 Kit,
but because Linda, Karil and Ivan are **service clients**, and service work
means contracts, invoices and liability.

> **⚠️ No session can file this for him.** It carries his signature, his home
> address and his personal liability. No Claude session can reach his computer,
> and none should file a legal registration in his name if it could. What this
> file does is make it a twenty-minute job instead of an afternoon of guessing.

> **⚠️ This is not legal or tax advice.** Everything below is public
> information from Florida's own site, verified on the date shown. The two
> decisions marked **ASK A PROFESSIONAL** genuinely need one.

## The numbers — verified 2026-08-18 at dos.fl.gov

| Item | Cost | When |

| Annual report | **$138.75** | Every year, **Jan 1 – May 1** |
| Late annual report | **+$400 penalty** | After May 1. Do not miss this. |
| Certified copy *(optional)* | $30.00 | Some banks ask for one |
| Certificate of Status *(optional)* | $5.00 | Rarely needed |

Filed online at **efile.sunbiz.org**. Re-check the fees at filing time — this
table is a log, not a live figure.

## The order of operations

1. **File the LLC** at `efile.sunbiz.org`. You need: a company name, a
   principal address, a **registered agent with a Florida street address**
   (this can be Isaac himself, at his own address — no PO box), and the
   member/manager names.
2. **Get the EIN from the IRS.** Free, online, about ten minutes, issued
   immediately. `irs.gov` — never pay a third-party site for this.
3. **Open a business bank account** using the LLC documents and the EIN.
4. **Move Stripe onto the business.** Payouts should land in the business
   account, not his personal one. This is the step that actually separates
   business money from personal money, which is the whole point.

**ASK A PROFESSIONAL — how the LLC is taxed.** A single-member LLC is taxed as
a sole proprietorship by default; an S-corp election can change that. Which is
better depends on his actual income and his other circumstances. **A CPA
answers this in one short conversation and it is worth the call.** No AI should
pick it for him.

## If Scott wants a piece later

Isaac's instinct, 2026-08-18, referencing the McDonald's secretary who took
stock instead of a raise: *"I'm not gonna pay him. He can have a percentage of
the company for whatever work he does — or whatever part of that product, not
the whole company."*

**The second half of that sentence is the right one, and it matters more than
it sounds.**

- **Company equity** makes Scott a *member of the LLC*. Members get rights —
  to information, to a say, sometimes to block things. Given Isaac's own read
  that Scott *"wants more power than he deserves,"* this is the exact
  structure to avoid.
- **A revenue share on one product** is just a contract: if that product earns,
  he gets an agreed cut of it. No membership, no rights over anything else,
  and it ends when the product ends.

Same upside for Scott. Far less exposure for Isaac. **ASK A PROFESSIONAL
before signing either one** — a short written agreement reviewed once is
cheaper than the alternative, and this is a thirty-five-year friendship.

## What is already true and needs no filing

Control does not come from paperwork here. Anything Scott or anyone else builds
runs on **Isaac's** machine — the video engine, the faceless pipeline, the
posting system, this repo. Owning the system is the control. The LLC is for
banking, contracts and liability, not for holding a position he already holds.
