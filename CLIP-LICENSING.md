## 1. Frame this correctly

Licensing pays for **scarce, one-in-a-million events** — the thing no one else
filmed. Cute dog-and-kitten domestic footage is the single most abundant supply
category on the internet. Jukin owns The Pet Collective, which is the closest
category fit, and they are not short of submissions.

**This is a free lottery ticket, not a revenue plan.** Do not build a forecast
on it. Do not put a licensing number in any pipeline doc, dashboard, or
projection. If a future session is tempted to model this, the answer is no.

The reason to do it at all is that a submission costs an email and the downside
is bounded — *provided* the rules below hold. The rules are what keep the
downside bounded. They are the whole point.

## 2. The terms, and what they actually gate

From the ViralHog contract as relayed by Ike:

| Term | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Licensor gets 50% of profit actually received | Ike's balance is half of what the agency nets, not half of headline license fees |
| Payouts held until balance exceeds $75 | **Gross profit received on Ike's clips must exceed ~$150 before a single dollar is due** |
| Partners take 75+ days to pay | Add a quarter-plus of lag on top of that, after the sale |
| If the balance never crosses $75 | No payment ever comes due. The clip is licensed; nothing is owed |

That last row is the one that matters. The realistic outcome for abundant-category
footage is that the balance never crosses the threshold — the agency holds a
license to the clip and Ike is paid nothing, permanently. That is not a failure
mode to guard against; it is the expected case. Accept it going in or don't submit.

The $150 figure is a **gate**, not a projection. It is the number that has to be
cleared before the payout question even opens. Nobody should present it as
expected revenue.

## 3. Hard rules

### Rule 1 — NON-EXCLUSIVE only

Never sign an exclusive deal. On an exclusive, the agency becomes the clip's
**agent**, with standing to issue takedowns. Creators have had their *own*
reposts pulled off their *own* accounts under this mechanism.

Weigh that against what the channel is: Instagram is the ~10x channel and the
offer-carrying surface. A takedown against @tysonstime is a catastrophic
downside traded for a lottery-ticket upside. Non-exclusive, in writing, or walk.

If a submission form has no non-exclusive option, that is a no. Do not submit and
"sort it out later" — the grant happens on submit.

### Rule 2 — NEVER submit footage the channel depends on

The rescue-story clips are the **offer-carrying lane** — 9.35 subs per 1k views.
That lane funds the Kit and the Amazon picks. It is the business.

Do not risk it. Concretely, the following are permanently off the table
regardless of any other consideration:

- The Titan / shelter-ID / "nobody came" origin material
- Anything on the second-chance, returned-once, or years-in-a-kennel beats
- Anything currently or plausibly carrying the Kit link as its offer vehicle

This rule bites harder than it first looks. Tyson's Time *is* the rescue channel
(@tysonstravels_rescuepitslife). The rescue framing is not a subset of the
library — it is the library's spine. Rule 2 removes the highest-narrative-value
clips, which are exactly the ones an agency would want most. That tension is
intentional and it resolves in favor of the channel, every time.

### Rule 3 — UNPUBLISHED clips only

Already posted to YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, or Threads = worth far less.
Buyers want first rights. A published clip is not worth the paperwork.

**The trap:** "unpublished file" is not the same as "unpublished moment." Buyers
care about the moment, not the file hash. Submitting the full-length original of
a clip whose best 8 seconds already shipped as a reel is still a published
moment, and misrepresenting it is a warranty problem, not just a pricing one.
Screen on the moment.

### Rule 4 — Clean audio rights

No music Ike doesn't own. Rights must be clean end to end. This includes:

- Licensed/trending audio added in-app (TikTok and Reels audio is **not** clearable)
- Music audible in the background of the original recording — a radio, a TV, a
  passing car, a store's overhead system
- Any track added during editing that isn't owned outright or genuinely royalty-free
  with a retained license record

If in doubt about background audio, the clip is submittable **muted** or not at all.

## 7. Pre-submission checklist

Every box, every clip, every time. One unchecked box = do not submit.

- [ ] Non-exclusive terms confirmed in writing (Rule 1)
- [ ] Not rescue-story / offer-carrying material (Rule 2)
- [ ] Never published on YT / IG / TikTok / Threads — **including native in-app
      posts** — and the *moment* is unpublished, not just the file (Rule 3)
- [ ] No third-party music, in-app audio, or background music in the recording (Rule 4)
- [ ] Selection based on Ike's written description, not on any claim of having
      watched the footage
- [ ] Ike has explicitly approved this specific clip for this specific agency
- [ ] Expectations recorded as a lottery ticket — no forecast built, anywhere
