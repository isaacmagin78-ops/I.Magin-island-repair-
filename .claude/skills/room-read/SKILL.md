# Room read

**Why this exists.** Isaac asked what to wear before going out, then named the
real point:

> *"If we're selling a personal assistant for Arturo or anybody of his ilk —
> anywhere in the world, wants to change their travel or do anything they want —
> that needs to be built in."*

**Anyone can build "book my flight." Knowing how a room works is the thing worth
paying for.** This skill is the working prototype of that feature. Every time it
runs well for Isaac, it is a spec for the product.

---

## The output — always this shape

He is usually getting dressed, or in a car, ten minutes out. Five lines, phone-readable.

```
[VENUE / EVENT] — [DATE]

THE DOOR      what will actually get someone turned away. Lead with this.
WEAR          specific enough to pull from a closet without thinking
THE ROOM      how it really runs — timing, who's there, how people talk
CURRENT       what reads as now versus dated, this season
DON'T         the one mistake that marks someone as not belonging
```

**"THE DOOR" always goes first.** Being turned away is the only genuinely
unrecoverable outcome. Everything else is a matter of degree.

---

## Rules

1. **Never guess a dress code.** A confidently wrong answer gets him turned away
   at a door, which is strictly worse than "I couldn't confirm it — here's the
   safe version." Search the venue's own site and recent reviews first.
2. **Say what is confirmed and what is inference.** "Their site states a collared
   shirt is required" is different from "venues of this type usually require it."
   Label the difference every time.
3. **Dress for the actual conditions.** South Florida, August: heat and humidity
   are the constraint. Thin fabrics, breathable, nothing that needs a jacket to
   look finished.
4. **Account for mobility.** He has limited mobility. **Prefer loafers and
   slip-ons over lace-ups**, and never suggest anything that requires standing a
   long time to get into. Say it as a style call, not a limitation.
5. **Fit is the whole game.** A cheap garment that fits beats an expensive one
   that doesn't. Say so when it's relevant, not every time.
6. **No lecture.** Five lines. He is walking out the door.

---

## What "CURRENT" means, and how to keep it honest

Fashion is the one lane in this workspace where being a season stale is visible
to everyone in the room. **Search it every time — do not answer from memory.**

Confirmed for **men's upscale nightlife, South Florida, August 2026:**

- **Jewel tones are what reads as current** — deep emerald, sapphire, burgundy,
  plum, rich teal. Black and navy remain permanently safe but read as neutral,
  not as an active choice.
- Thin fabrics — linen and light blends — are correct for the climate and for
  the season.
- Collared shirt, dark denim or tailored trousers, dress shoes or polished
  loafers is the working baseline at upscale venues.
- **No sneakers, no shorts, no athletic wear** at anywhere calling itself
  upscale. This is the enforced part, not the advisory part.

*(Sourced 2026-08-16. Re-verify before repeating after roughly November — the
South Florida season turns and so does what reads as current.)*

**Isaac's own standing note:** he has thirty years in these rooms and knows that
jeans, a t-shirt and the right shoes can outperform a suit in the right venue.
**When he says something contradicts what you found, he is the authority and you
are not.** Record what he says; do not argue him out of it.

## Beyond clothes

The full version of this — the one that becomes the product — also covers:
arrival timing, who is likely in the room, how the check works, tipping norms,
what to have ready before walking in, and how conversation is actually opened at
that kind of event. **Answer whichever of these the situation calls for**, in the
same five-line discipline.
