# Market read, on demand

**Why this exists.** Isaac, 2026-08-16:

> *"I have to ask you guys for updates. I should be on top of it and know what's
> going on in the world... If someone wants to know what current industries are,
> if I need to analyze what the market is for a golf course or a yacht club — on
> demand."*

He is often standing in front of the person he is talking about the business
with. **The deliverable is something he can say out loud in the next two
minutes**, not a report he reads later.

---

## The output — always exactly this shape

Six lines. Never more. He reads slower than you write, and he may be reading
this on a phone in a loud room.

```
[BUSINESS / INDUSTRY] — read as of [DATE]

THE NUMBER      one figure that frames the whole thing, with its source and year
THE DIRECTION   growing / flat / shrinking, and how fast
THE PRESSURE    the thing quietly squeezing this business right now
THE OPENING     where someone with his skills could actually get paid
THE QUESTION    one thing to ask the owner that shows he did the homework
```

**"THE QUESTION" is the most valuable line.** He is usually in a conversation,
not writing a memo. A question that lands proves competence faster than any
figure. Make it specific enough that only someone who looked would ask it.

---

## Rules — these are not negotiable

1. **Every number carries a source and a year.** If it cannot be sourced, write
   `unsourced` and give it anyway — a labeled rumor he can check beats silence.
2. **Never invent a figure.** Not a market size, not a growth rate, not a member
   count, not a price. An honest "I don't have that" is always the better answer.
   This is the single hardest rule in this workspace and it applies fully here.
3. **Local beats national.** A national golf-industry figure is background. What
   matters is Broward and Palm Beach County. Search locally first.
4. **Plain language, no jargon.** If a term is unavoidable, define it in the same
   sentence. "TAM," "CAGR," and "headwinds" are all failures here.
5. **Name the date out loud.** A finding is only as fresh as its date, and he may
   repeat this to a real person who can check it.
6. **Never hand him homework.** No "you should look into." Do the looking.

---

## How to run it

1. **Pin the subject.** A specific venue, or the industry, or both — a named club
   in Pompano is a different search from "the yacht club industry."
2. **Search in this order:** local news and county business press → industry
   association and trade publications → the business's own site and reviews →
   national context last.
3. **Look for the pressure**, because it is what nobody volunteers: aging
   membership, deferred maintenance, insurance costs (enormous in South Florida
   right now), staffing, a competitor that opened nearby, a special assessment.
4. **Find the opening honestly.** Isaac's real capabilities are: video and film
   production, marketing content systems, listing and campaign packages,
   automated social posting, and concierge-style done-for-you work. **If there is
   no honest fit, say so.** A forced angle is worse than "not your market."
5. **Write the six lines. Stop.** Offer more only if he asks.

## ⭐ Where it goes — this step is not optional

**Corrected 2026-08-18 on Isaac's go-ahead.** This section used to point at
`TREND-WATCH.md`. **That was wrong and it never happened once** — `TREND-WATCH.md` is
about the creator and AI lane, so a read on a yacht club had nowhere to sit and every
market read ever run evaporated. A rule pointing at the wrong file is the same as no rule.

**Append every market read to `concierge-systems/MARKET-LOG.md`**, newest at the bottom,
using the block defined in that file. Then mirror anything that changes strategy into
Ike OS in Notion.

What to capture beyond the six lines:

1. **Why he asked** — who he was standing in front of. This turns the log into a map of
   his real pipeline, which stays useful long after the figures die.
2. **Split DURABLE from PERISHABLE.** How the business runs is true next year; this
   year's numbers are not. **Stamp a re-check date and never repeat a perishable line to
   a real person without re-verifying it.**
3. **⭐ Isaac's correction, verbatim.** He has thirty years around these businesses and
   the research does not. **His word overrides the research** — record it, do not argue.
4. **What happened.** Did the question land? Was the pressure line right? A read that
   reality corrected is worth more than one that was never tested.
5. **"Not his market" is a finding too.** Write it down so the next session does not
   re-derive a forced angle.

**Do not make him do this.** He is mid-conversation — give him the six lines, then write
the entry yourself. Never hand him the logging as homework.

**Never deliver only in chat.** That is how nine months of research evaporated.

## South Florida standing context

Reuse this instead of re-deriving it every time. **Re-verify anything load-bearing
before repeating it to a real person.**

- Isaac operates in **Pompano Beach**; his working radius is Broward and Palm
  Beach County, with Miami-Dade reachable.
- **Property insurance and reinsurance costs dominate every waterfront and
  club-adjacent business here.** It is the pressure line more often than not.
- Seasonality is severe: the season runs roughly November through April.
  August is the trough — which changes what "slow" means in any figure.
- Clubs, marinas and HOAs in this market skew toward an older membership and
  toward deferred capital projects funded by special assessment.
- He already has a real-estate content system built and a video engine that has
  delivered paid client work. Those are the two strongest honest openings.
